An open standard · Governed by public specifications

Connectivity by name, contract, and context.

CNS/CP — the Connectivity Naming System with Connection Profiles — is an open standard for secure, composable, and orchestrated connectivity between digital systems. Nodes interact through named profiles in specific contexts, moving beyond classic Internet models like DNS and IP addresses.

Every node declares its role as provider or consumer of a Connection Profile — uniquely named like cp:test.abc — and connects on compatible context: a smart building, a fleet, a digital service.

Read the specification Learn how it works

The model in four steps

Traditional networks connect addresses and hope the rules get bolted on later. CNS/CP is relationship-centric: nothing connects without declared identity, roles, and a matching Connection Profile. Others standardize messages — CNS/CP standardizes binding.

1 · Declare

Nodes publish the Connection Profiles they provide or consume, inside a declared context.

2 · Broker

An orchestrator matches compatible provider and consumer declarations in a shared context.

3 · Bind

A governed connection is instantiated — validated, policy-enforced, and auditable from the start.

4 · Operate

The contract is monitored, adjusted, and audited across the connection’s whole lifecycle.

Orchestration at the heart

In CNS/CP, an orchestrator matches system nodes based on their declared capabilities and context, enacting automated and dynamic workflows. This simplifies interoperability, fosters smarter system relationships, and elevates network trust, resilience, and agility.

CNS/CP is fully open source and governed by public specifications. Whether you are building, connecting, or governing digital systems, CNS/CP is your pathway to seamless integration and scalable value.
