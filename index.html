An open standard · Governed by public specifications

Connectivity by name, contract, and context.

CNS/CP — the Connectivity Naming System using Connection Profiles — is an open standard for deciding whether two digital systems may connect, and on what terms. Systems connect by what they declare they can do, at named Contexts, under a Realm’s published rules, rather than by address.

Every Node declares itself Provider or Consumer of a Connection Profile — uniquely named like cp:example.occupancy — at a Context: a room, a vehicle, a service. Where two declarations fit, the Realm’s Governor forms the Connection.

Six steps, two phases

Traditional networks connect addresses and hope the rules get bolted on later. In CNS/CP nothing is connected unless a Node has been admitted, holds standing at a Context, and has declared a Capability that another declaration matches. Others standardize messages — CNS/CP standardizes binding.

1 · Enroll

A Node presents an identity, and the Governor admits or rejects it.

2 · Authorize

The Node asserts a Context, and the Governor authorizes or rejects the assertion.

3 · Declare

The Node declares a Capability: a Connection Profile, a role in it, and an authorized Context.

4 · Reconcile

Whenever anything changes, the Governor brings the Realm’s Connections back in line with what it holds.

5 · Match

Same Profile, complementary roles, matching Contexts, a version both support: every pair that fits is matched.

6 · Bind

The Governor forms the Connection. Everything it carries passes through the Realm.

A Governor at the heart of every Realm

Each Realm — a building, a fleet, a hospital — has one Governor. It holds the Realm’s published rules and its Context Register, decides who is admitted, and forms every Connection. Given the same declarations, the same published rules and the same Profile versions, any conforming Governor forms the same Connections. Realms compose: a containing Realm sees a Node, not the Realm behind it.

CNS/CP is open: the specification is public, the Registry is public, and the reference implementation is open source under Apache 2.0. Whether you are building, connecting, or governing digital systems, CNS/CP is your pathway to seamless integration and scalable value.
